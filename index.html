The House of All Faiths & Communities is a non-partisan body bringing together faith, community, civic and cultural leaders from across the United Kingdom. We unite around three principles: respect, responsibility and accountability, to stand against fear and discrimination, strengthen the bonds between communities, preserve the cultures and traditions we inherit, and hold ourselves publicly accountable for the commitments we make and the actions we take.

What we are

HOFAC is a house of faith, community, cultural and civic leadership, working towards peace, harmony and responsible coexistence. It is aligned with no single religion, party or community, and asks no one to set aside what they believe.

Britain's cultural inheritance is not held by any one community. It is carried by all of them, and it is preserved when they take responsibility for it together. HOFAC exists to make that responsibility visible.

Annual commitments

Each member undertakes at least one piece of practical work a year and gives an account of what came of it. Both are published.

A balanced house

HOFAC is of most use when no single tradition or community predominates within it. Membership and invitations are managed with that balance in mind.

A place to contribute

HOFAC is not a platform for promoting a member's own organisation or interests. Members join for the work that is held in common.

Institutions, not individuals

An organisation holds its membership through its head or senior representative. When that person changes, the membership passes to their successor.

Dialogue is where this work begins, not where it ends.

Our vision

A world in which people live without fear of discrimination or violence; where faiths, cultures and communities coexist with dignity; where the cultural inheritance of nations is protected; and where those who lead accept responsibility not only for their own communities, but for the wellbeing of society as a whole.

Diversity in unity. Humanity with responsibility.

HOFAC begins in the United Kingdom. The vision is not limited to it.

The principles that hold us

RESPECT

We respect people of every religion, belief, background and viewpoint. We accept that people of good faith will disagree, and we hold that lawful disagreement is part of a free society. We reject hatred, intimidation and the dehumanising of any community.

RESPONSIBILITY

Faith and community leadership carries a responsibility beyond one's own community. We accept a duty to strengthen civic society, to uphold peaceful coexistence and the rule of law, to contribute to the common good, and to preserve the cultural inheritance we hold together.

ACCOUNTABILITY

We hold ourselves to what we have said. Every organisation that joins HOFAC makes a commitment to action each year, and reports openly on what it delivered. We publish those commitments and their outcomes.

We do not ask for agreement in matters of belief. We ask for responsibility in matters of civic life.

Neutrality is our strength

HOFAC does not exist to advance one religion, political ideology, community or faction over another.

Neutrality here is not caution or evasion. It is the condition that allows communities who disagree with one another to sit in the same room, and it is held to deliberately.

We believe a country's culture can be cared for without defining ourselves against any community within it.

It is a test we apply to what we publish, to those we invite, and to the platforms we lend our name to.

Preserving what we hold in common

The cultures, traditions, languages and histories of these islands are an inheritance in trust. So are the traditions that communities have brought with them and made part of British life.

HOFAC works to preserve both. We support the communities of the four nations of the United Kingdom in sustaining their own heritage, and we work to ensure that inheritance is understood, valued and passed on, together with the shared civic values that make harmonious community life possible: democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, and mutual respect and tolerance of different faiths and beliefs.

Preservation is not nostalgia. A culture is kept alive by the people who practise it, teach it and carry it forward. Our work is to help communities do exactly that, and to do it in respect of one another.

About HOFAC

The House of All Faiths & Communities was registered in August 2026 as a Scottish Charitable Incorporated Organisation, charity number SC055598.

HOFAC works across the whole of the United Kingdom. Our charitable registration is currently held in Scotland, and we will extend our registration elsewhere in the UK as our work develops.

It was founded on a straightforward observation. Britain's faith and community organisations do a great deal of good, and much of it is invisible. At the same time, the cultural inheritance those communities hold in common is under pressure, from neglect as much as from division.

HOFAC was created to address both. We bring communities together around a shared commitment to respect, responsibility and accountability, and we make the resulting work visible.

Our purposes

The following is the wording of HOFAC's charitable objects as they appear on the Scottish Charity Register.

Promotion of religious and racial harmony, equality and mutual respect

To promote religious and racial harmony, equality, inclusion and mutual respect among people of different faiths, beliefs and communities in the United Kingdom, and to support responsible peaceful coexistence and community cohesion, in particular (but not exclusively) by:

  1. (a)facilitating structured dialogue, consultation and cooperation between faith and community leaders;
  2. (b)promoting shared understanding, constructive community relations and peaceful engagement;
  3. (c)advancing education and public awareness to reduce misunderstanding, prejudice and conflict;
  4. (d)producing and disseminating guidance, research and publications that support good practice in community cohesion and mutual respect; and
  5. (e)supporting inclusive participation in civic and cultural life in a manner consistent with the organisation's independence and political neutrality.

Advancement of arts, culture and heritage

To advance the arts, culture and heritage of the United Kingdom by promoting the understanding, appreciation, preservation and responsible stewardship of:

  1. (a)the UK's cultural heritage and traditions, recognising the diverse national cultures of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland and their shared history; and
  2. (b)shared civic values that underpin harmonious community life, including (without limitation) democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, and mutual respect and tolerance of different faiths and beliefs, including (without limitation) through:
  3. (c)education, learning resources and public programmes;
  4. (d)public engagement, exhibitions, events, cultural initiatives and partnerships that strengthen cohesion and shared belonging;
  5. (e)research and the publication of reports, guidance and materials;
  6. (f)initiatives that support the responsible stewardship and celebration of heritage, traditions and cultural expression for public benefit; and
  7. (g)facilitating structured dialogue, consultation and cooperation between faith and community leaders.

Our symbol

The Ligature of Faiths brings together three elements. The crown signifies both the heavens and authority, its sun drawn on the angles of the Union Jack. The linked chain, drawn in a circle, represents strength and connection. At the centre, the eternal knot represents infinite wisdom, compassion, interconnectedness and the eternal cycle of life.

Independence

HOFAC belongs to all of the communities that take part in it. It is not affiliated to any one faith, organisation or political party, and it does not require any participating organisation to share the beliefs of any other. We do not ask for agreement in matters of belief. We ask for responsibility in matters of civic life.

Governance

HOFAC is governed by its board of trustees under Scottish charity law and is accountable to the Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator. Its entry on the Scottish Charity Register can be viewed at the OSCR website.

Guiding principles · in development

The HOFAC Charter

The principles set out below indicate the ground on which HOFAC is being built. They are offered as a guide, so that those considering joining us can see plainly what we hold to.

The founding Charter will broadly align with these principles. It will be drawn up with our founding members, ratified by the House at the founding assembly in London, and published in full thereafter.

Respect for every person

We respect people of every religion, belief, background and viewpoint, and accept them as they are. People of good faith will disagree, and lawful disagreement is part of a free society.

Peace without exception

We reject violence, intimidation, hatred and discrimination in thought, word and action. Where there is disagreement, we look for resolution through dialogue, reason and compassion.

Protection of one another

We ask that our own communities do no harm to others. Where a community is threatened, we stand together for its protection, whether or not it is our own.

Care for culture and heritage

We hold the cultures, traditions, languages and histories of these islands as an inheritance in trust, together with the traditions that communities have brought and made part of British life. We respect the culture and the laws of the country in which we work.

Responsibility beyond our own

Leadership carries a responsibility wider than one's own community. Each member undertakes practical work each year and gives an account of it.

Neutrality

HOFAC advances no religion, political party, ideology or faction above another, and cares for culture without defining itself against any community.

Contribution before self-interest

HOFAC is a place to contribute. It is not for advancing a member's own organisation or interests, and not for selling or recruiting.

Beneath all of them sit the three principles that HOFAC exists to practise: respect, responsibility and accountability.

Founder & Inspiration

HOFAC arose from Mohanji's vision of a platform through which leaders and communities could work together for harmony, responsible coexistence, the preservation of culture, and a kinder society. He asked that the work begin in Britain, and that it be open to everyone willing to take responsibility for the society they share.

HOFAC is an independent Scottish charity, democratically governed by its board of trustees. It is not affiliated to any single religion, organisation or political party. Inspired by Mohanji's vision; independently and democratically governed.

In his words

The work of our generation is to transform humankind into kind humans, in a world where no fear exists.

Neutrality is our strength. We are like the sun, and like nature, which never discriminates between species.

Culture is the spine of a nation. The many flavours of a country are what make the country.

We stand beyond religion, language, caste, creed, country, culture and colour. We stand with humanity, and with human responsibility.

These passages are drawn from Mohanji's guidance to the trustees on the purpose of HOFAC.

Apply to join

HOFAC is open to those who lead and represent communities in the United Kingdom: faith leaders, community and cultural organisations, civic institutions, youth organisations, and others whose work brings people together.

Membership is held by an organisation through its head or senior representative. You are welcome to apply on behalf of your organisation, or to write to us first if you would prefer to talk it through.

By application

Any organisation whose purpose and conduct sit comfortably alongside our principles may apply to join. Applications are considered by the board.

By invitation

We also invite organisations and individuals directly. Invitations are made with the balance of the House in mind, so that no single tradition or community predominates.

Privacy

The House of All Faiths & Communities (HOFAC) respects your privacy.

If you contact us through this website or by email, we will use the details you provide only to respond to your enquiry and to correspond with you about the matter you raise. We do not share your details with any other organisation, and we do not use them for marketing.
