A common inheritance,
a shared responsibility.

The House of All Faiths & Communities is a non-partisan body bringing together faith, community, civic and cultural leaders from across the United Kingdom. We unite around three principles: responsibility, accountability and respect (RAR), to stand against fear and discrimination, strengthen the bonds between communities, preserve the cultures and traditions we inherit, and hold ourselves publicly accountable for the commitments we make and the actions we take.

What we are

We must speak when the public cannot. This is why HOFAC was formed.

HOFAC is a house of faith, community, cultural and civic leadership, working towards peace, harmony and responsible coexistence. It is aligned with no single religion, party or community, and asks no one to set aside what they believe.

Britain's cultural inheritance is not held by any one community. It is carried by all of them, and it is preserved when they take responsibility for it together. HOFAC exists to make that responsibility visible.

Annual commitments

Each member undertakes at least one piece of practical work a year and gives an account of what came of it. Both are published.

A balanced house

HOFAC is of most use when no single tradition or community predominates within it. Membership and invitations are managed with that balance in mind.

A place to contribute

HOFAC is not a platform for promoting a member's own organisation or interests. Members join for the work that is held in common.

Institutions, not individuals

An organisation holds its membership through its head or senior representative. When that person changes, the membership passes to their successor.

Dialogue is where this work begins, not where it ends.

Our vision

A world in which people live without fear of discrimination or violence; where faiths, cultures and communities coexist with dignity; where the cultural inheritance of nations is protected; and where those who lead accept responsibility not only for their own communities, but for the wellbeing of society as a whole.

Diversity in unity. Humanity with responsibility.

HOFAC begins in the United Kingdom. The vision is not limited to it.

The principles that hold us

RESPONSIBILITY

Faith and community leadership carries a responsibility beyond one's own community. We accept a duty to strengthen civic society, to uphold peaceful coexistence and the rule of law, to contribute to the common good, and to preserve the cultural inheritance we hold together.

ACCOUNTABILITY

We hold ourselves to what we have said. Every organisation that joins HOFAC makes a commitment to action each year, and reports openly on what it delivered. We publish those commitments and their outcomes.

RESPECT

We respect people of every religion, belief, background and viewpoint. We accept that people of good faith will disagree, and we hold that lawful disagreement is part of a free society. We reject hatred, intimidation and the dehumanising of any community.

We do not ask for agreement in matters of belief. We ask for responsibility in matters of civic life.

Neutrality is our strength

HOFAC does not exist to advance one religion, political ideology, community or faction over another.

Neutrality here is not caution or evasion. It is the condition that allows communities who disagree with one another to sit in the same room, and it is held to deliberately.

We believe a country's culture can be cared for without defining ourselves against any community within it.

It is a test we apply to what we publish, to those we invite, and to the platforms we lend our name to.

Preserving what we hold in common

The cultures, traditions, languages and histories of these islands are an inheritance in trust. So are the traditions that communities have brought with them and made part of British life.

HOFAC works to preserve both. We support the communities of the four nations of the United Kingdom in sustaining their own heritage, and we work to ensure that inheritance is understood, valued and passed on, together with the shared civic values that make harmonious community life possible: democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, and mutual respect and tolerance of different faiths and beliefs.

Preservation is not nostalgia. A culture is kept alive by the people who practise it, teach it and carry it forward. Our work is to help communities do exactly that, and to do it in respect of one another.

About HOFAC

The House of All Faiths & Communities was registered in August 2026 as a Scottish Charitable Incorporated Organisation, charity number SC055598.

HOFAC works across the whole of the United Kingdom. Our charitable registration is currently held in Scotland, and we will extend our registration elsewhere in the UK as our work develops.

It was founded on a straightforward observation. Britain's faith and community organisations do a great deal of good, and much of it is invisible. At the same time, the cultural inheritance those communities hold in common is under pressure, from neglect as much as from division.

HOFAC was created to address both. We bring communities together around a shared commitment to responsibility, accountability and respect (RAR), and we make the resulting work visible.

Our purposes

The following is the wording of HOFAC's charitable objects as they appear on the Scottish Charity Register.

Promotion of religious and racial harmony, equality and mutual respect

To promote religious and racial harmony, equality, inclusion and mutual respect among people of different faiths, beliefs and communities in the United Kingdom, and to support responsible peaceful coexistence and community cohesion, in particular (but not exclusively) by:

  1. (a)facilitating structured dialogue, consultation and cooperation between faith and community leaders;
  2. (b)promoting shared understanding, constructive community relations and peaceful engagement;
  3. (c)advancing education and public awareness to reduce misunderstanding, prejudice and conflict;
  4. (d)producing and disseminating guidance, research and publications that support good practice in community cohesion and mutual respect; and
  5. (e)supporting inclusive participation in civic and cultural life in a manner consistent with the organisation's independence and political neutrality.

Advancement of arts, culture and heritage

To advance the arts, culture and heritage of the United Kingdom by promoting the understanding, appreciation, preservation and responsible stewardship of:

  1. (a)the UK's cultural heritage and traditions, recognising the diverse national cultures of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland and their shared history; and
  2. (b)shared civic values that underpin harmonious community life, including (without limitation) democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, and mutual respect and tolerance of different faiths and beliefs, including (without limitation) through:
  3. (c)education, learning resources and public programmes;
  4. (d)public engagement, exhibitions, events, cultural initiatives and partnerships that strengthen cohesion and shared belonging;
  5. (e)research and the publication of reports, guidance and materials;
  6. (f)initiatives that support the responsible stewardship and celebration of heritage, traditions and cultural expression for public benefit; and
  7. (g)facilitating structured dialogue, consultation and cooperation between faith and community leaders.

Our symbol

The Ligature of Faiths brings together three elements. The crown signifies both the heavens and authority, its sun drawn on the angles of the Union Jack. The linked chain, drawn in a circle, represents strength and connection. At the centre, the eternal knot represents infinite wisdom, compassion, interconnectedness and the eternal cycle of life.

Independence

HOFAC belongs to all of the communities that take part in it. It is not affiliated to any one faith, organisation or political party, and it does not require any participating organisation to share the beliefs of any other. We do not ask for agreement in matters of belief. We ask for responsibility in matters of civic life.

Governance

HOFAC is governed by its board of trustees under Scottish charity law and is accountable to the Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator. Its entry on the Scottish Charity Register can be viewed at the OSCR website.

Founder & Inspiration

Mohanji

“The work of our generation is to transform humankind into kind humans, in a world where no fear exists.”

Dr. Brahmarishi Mohanji is a globally recognised humanitarian, philanthropist and spiritual leader dedicated to transforming humankind into kind humans through compassion, non-violence, responsibility and selfless service.

He is the founder of several international organisations, including Mohanji Foundation, Ammucare Charitable Trust, ACT Foundation, the World Consciousness Alliance and the World Tribal Alliance, advancing humanitarian action, interfaith harmony and cultural preservation worldwide. His global work has received numerous honours, including the BRICS-CCI Global Compassion Ambassador Award for Lifetime Achievement in 2024.

The origin of HOFAC

HOFAC arose from Mohanji's vision of a platform through which leaders and communities could work together for harmony, responsible coexistence, the preservation of culture, and a kinder society. He asked that the work begin in Britain, and that it be open to everyone willing to take responsibility for the society they share.

In his words

Neutrality is our strength. We are like the sun, and like nature, which never discriminates between species.

Culture is the spine of a nation. The many flavours of a country are what make the country.

We stand beyond religion, language, caste, creed, country, culture and colour. We stand with humanity, and with human responsibility.

Acceptance is the norm. We accept people as they are, and we renounce violence and discrimination in every form.

HOFAC is an independent Scottish charity, democratically governed by its board of trustees. It is not affiliated to any single religion, organisation or political party. Inspired by Mohanji's vision; independently and democratically governed.

The HOFAC Declaration

“The world suffers a lot, not just because of the violence of the bad people, but because of the silence of the good people.”

We must speak when the public cannot. This is why HOFAC was formed.

We, the faith leaders of the world must unite to ensure peace and sanctity of the world. We must, together, take the pledge that the people who follow our faith will never harm or hurt any person, any being or any property of our respective societies.

We have to unite all the faiths and communities of our country, our region, our world with love, harmony, kindness, selflessness, nonviolence and togetherness. We must, as faith and community leaders, uphold Responsibility, Accountability and Respect in our societies. We must distance ourselves from hypocrisy of all kinds and should be bold enough to speak out loud when any people of any community disrespect our pledge of harmony and solidarity.

We have a great responsibility to maintain peace and harmony, love, hope and compassion, while following our chosen faiths, without discrimination. We are united as love and kindness incarnate. Irrespective of our faith as well as political inclination, we shall only respond with love and understanding of every situation of the society. Nobody is higher or lower. Nobody is better or worse. We have to protect the helpless strata of our society. We have to denounce violence of all kinds. We have to quarantine the harmful of our society irrespective of their faith and political inclinations. We have to always maintain righteousness, harmony and peace in our societies.

We are HOFAC. We are responsible faith leaders without hierarchies.

We are all responsible for the well being as well as the conduct of people of our faith whom we lead. We are here to ensure everyone who is born on earth has a justified existence and a complete life.

Advisors to The House

Advisors to The House are senior figures from faith, cultural and civic life who lend HOFAC their counsel and their standing. Their role is to advise, to open doors, and to hold the House to the standards it has set for itself.

Goswami Sushil Ji Maharaj

Advisor to The House

Maharishi Bhrigu Peethadhishwar · National Convenor of the Bhartiya Sarva Dharma Sansad

Goswami Sushil Ji Maharaj is Maharishi Bhrigu Peethadhishwar and National Convenor of the Bhartiya Sarva Dharma Sansad, an organisation devoted to fostering peace and harmony through interfaith dialogue.

Representing India internationally, he has taken part in major conferences in Chicago, London and Spain. He was honoured at the United Nations Assembly and at the HWPL Global Peace Leaders Conference in South Korea, where he received the Messenger of Peace award.

Born in Delhi in 1945, he is also an acclaimed director, choreographer and composer, known for his contributions to cultural programmes and Ramleela productions. Under his leadership an international inter-religious peace conference is to be convened in India.

Dame Gloria Starr Kins

Advisor to The House

President and Founder, Society & Diplomatic Review · United Nations Correspondent

Dame Gloria Starr Kins is President and Founder of Society & Diplomatic Review and a veteran correspondent at the United Nations. She has served as society and diplomatic editor of Irish Connections and the Irish Examiner, and as a correspondent for the Jewish Post.

Her career in publishing and broadcasting includes heading the New York office of Curtis Publishing — the Saturday Evening Post, Holiday and Status — as Managing Editor, editing Status Diplomat alongside Wyatt Cooper, leading United Nations operations for WOR Radio and Television, and associate producing NBC’s Open Mind.

She is Vice-Chair of the Center for Global Action for Sustainable Development and a founding member of UNICEF’s Manhattan chapter and of the Islamic Council of Europe. She serves as Government Liaison on the board of the OCCAM Infopoverty World Conference and heads the North American office of the Association de la Plume pour la Culture et le Développement.

Nazem Alkudsi, CFA

Advisor to The House

Formerly Chief Executive of Invest AD · Abu Dhabi Investment Authority · Long Arc News

Nazem Alkudsi brings four decades at the heart of global capital, and the conviction that the forces shaping the world are never purely financial: they are cultural, political and spiritual.

He spent nearly twenty years at the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, managing portfolios from Silicon Valley through the dot-com era to Tokyo during Japan’s lost decade, before serving as Chief Executive of Invest AD, deploying sovereign capital across the Middle East, North Africa and frontier markets. Throughout that career he observed that the most consequential influences on nations and markets were rooted in belief and culture, yet were rarely discussed in the rooms where decisions were made.

Through his writing at Long Arc News he examines how belief systems drive the behaviour of nations, and why some institutions think in generations while others struggle to see beyond the quarter. At HOFAC he brings a cross-cultural view of how faith and values meet economics and public life, and a long view of what it takes to build an institution meant to endure.

HOFAC Peace Ambassadors

HOFAC Peace Ambassadors are figures of distinguished standing in diplomacy and international affairs: men and women who have spent their working lives building understanding between nations, between institutions and between peoples.

They represent the House in the wider world, and hold those relations to the three principles the House itself is built upon: responsibility, accountability and respect.

Haji Syed Salman Chishty

HOFAC Peace Ambassador

Sufi tradition · Custodian family of Dargah Ajmer Sharif, the shrine of Khwaja Moinuddin Chishty

Haji Syed Salman Chishty was born into one of the world’s oldest living spiritual trusts. For eight hundred years his family has held the keys to the Sanctum Sanctorum of Dargah Ajmer Sharif, the shrine of the twelfth-century Sufi saint Khwaja Moinuddin Chishty, where some forty thousand pilgrims of every faith arrive each day and more than a million gather for the annual Urs.

For twenty years he has carried that tradition’s message — Love Towards All, Malice Towards None — far beyond Ajmer. He has given keynotes at the United Nations in New York and Geneva, at the G20 and BRICS forums, at ASEAN’s interfaith summit and at leading universities, and has been received as an honoured guest by Sikh, Hindu, Buddhist, Christian, Jewish and Muslim institutions alike.

The shrine he serves runs one of the largest continuous community kitchens in history, an estimated 146 million free meals over twenty years.

Ambassador Vladimir Božović, PhD

HOFAC Peace Ambassador

Consul General of the Republic of Serbia in New York · President of the Society of Foreign Consuls in New York · Chairman of the Global Consular Leadership Council of United World Leaders

Vladimir Božović served as Ambassador of the Republic of Serbia to Montenegro from 2019 to 2023, and is the first representative of Serbia to lead the Society of Foreign Consuls in New York in more than a century of its history.

He has held senior office in Serbian public administration as Assistant Minister of Internal Affairs and Inspector General, as State Secretary responsible for internal oversight and international cooperation, and as Adviser to the Prime Minister for regional cooperation and relations with religious communities.

A lawyer by training, he holds a doctorate from the Faculty of Security Studies at the University of Belgrade and teaches as an Assistant Professor in Belgrade. He has acted as legal counsel to the Serbian Orthodox Church and to the Metropolitanate of Montenegro and the Littoral in cases concerning fundamental human and civil rights, and served as National Coordinator for Police and Human Rights at the Council of Europe.

His work has consistently joined diplomacy with the rule of law, anti-corruption and the protection of human rights.
